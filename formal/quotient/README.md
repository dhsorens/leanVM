# Security of `flock::quotient`

A Lean 4 development over [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib) and VCVio for the proof system of `crates/flock/src/quotient.rs`, described in `doc/quotient/main.tex`. No `sorry`; the theorems named below depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, which the root module pins.

## What is proved

**Knowledge soundness.**

- `knowledgeSoundness` (`RandomOracle.lean`): the non-interactive proof in the random-oracle model. An adversary makes at most `Q` adaptive queries and outputs a statement and a proof; the verifier asks its three challenges of the same oracle. The probability that the verifier accepts while the cells read off the proof's first message do not satisfy the relation is at most `(Q + 3)` times the largest round error. `knowledgeSoundness_bits`: at the protocol's sizes (`q = 2^192`, `N = 2^22` cells, eight words, identities of degree at most `2^27`) that is `(Q + 3) / 2^128`. It is proved from the three round bounds below and VCVio's bound on fresh bad queries (`prEvent_randomOracle_le_of_bad_queries`), not from the next theorem: ArkLib states the implication from round-by-round soundness to the Fiat-Shamir transform with `sorry`.
- `verifier_rbrKnowledgeSoundness` (`RoundByRound.lean`): the interactive protocol is round-by-round knowledge sound in ArkLib's definition (`Verifier.rbrKnowledgeSoundness`, through its worst-case form), with errors `(2 N^2 + 4 N + words) / q`, `d / q` and `3 / q` for its three challenges, each at most `2^-128` at those sizes (`rbrError_le`).

**Completeness, of the algebra.** `Params.honest_consistent`, `Params.accepts_of_consistent` (`Completeness.lean`): given a first message whose cells satisfy the relation, the honest second message (the interpolation of the sum's values and the two quotients) is consistent with it wherever no denominator vanishes on a cell, and the four claims are then true at every challenge the verifier does not reject. These are two lemmas, not ArkLib's `completeness`: there is no probability statement, and the honest messages are not shown to lie within the degree bounds of the protocol whose soundness is proved. `Params.exists_witness` (`Witness.lean`): every assignment of the cells is the cells of some first message, over the one field of `Params`. It gives no degree bound on that message, and no message with coefficients in the small field, which the zero-knowledge theorems take as a hypothesis.

**Zero knowledge.**

- `zeroKnowledge` (`Distance.lean`): the non-interactive proof, in the random-oracle model with a simulator that programs the oracle. The oracle may already hold answers: the theorem is for each state `c₀`, each statement and each witness, so it covers whatever anyone asked before, and, by averaging over the state (not stated), a statement and a witness chosen from it. The real experiment draws masks and two handles, runs the honest prover against the oracle in that state, and outputs the proof, its verdict, its challenges and the oracle's new state. The simulator `simNI` takes the statement and the oracle's state only: it draws handles, challenges and four values (uniform in the cubic extension `E`, or in `K = GF(2^64)` when the challenge's row is in `K`), takes the verifier's own nonzero check for the verdict, and writes its challenges in the oracle. For every event on the outcome, the two experiments' probabilities differ by at most `zkBound = (2 N + 4 deg) / q + |U| / |H|`: `N` cells, a vanishing polynomial of degree `deg`, `q` field elements, `U` any set containing the handles the oracle was already asked about with this statement, `H` the handles; `exists_handles`: a state holding `Q` answers has such a `U` of at most `Q` handles (that `Q` queries leave at most `Q` answers is not stated). `zkError_le`: the first term is at most `2^-128` at the protocol's sizes (by hand it is about `2^-168`); the second is the chance a fresh commitment collides with an earlier query.
- Under it (`NonInteractive.lean`, `Simulation.lean`, `ZeroKnowledge.lean`): `realNI_eq_simNI`, the two experiments give exactly the same probability to every outcome whose first handle is fresh and whose challenges are good for the witness; `card_not_good_le`, few challenges are not good; `realView_eq_simView`, for fixed good challenges the honest verifier's whole view of the interactive proof has the simulator's distribution; `realValues_eq_simValues`, the four opened values are distributed as the simulator's wherever the vanishing polynomial is not zero, because three mask coefficients over `K` cover exactly one value in `E` (`maskedValue_bijective`), and one in `K`.

**What the relation means for a circuit.** `AdderCircuit.determined` (`Circuit.lean`): for a circuit of full adders with no loop (a row is an input, reading its own output and two zeros, or reads constants and rows of lower rank), two assignments of the rows' outputs, in any field of characteristic 2, that satisfy every gate and agree on the inputs agree everywhere, and every output is an element of `GF(4)`; an input row holds 0 or 1 (`input_is_bit`). So a satisfying assignment is the circuit's computation on some input bits: uniqueness is the theorem, and that the computation is a satisfying assignment is what the Rust tests check. With it, `copy_iterate` (a value is the same along a cycle of the wiring) and `word_pins` (a public word all of whose cells are one cell pins it, which is how word 5 fixes the zero; the one is then fixed by word 4, whose other cells are zeros, a step not stated in Lean).

**The parameters exist.** `Params.ofData`, `Params.ofData_exact` (`Instance.lean`): from rows whose cells are distinct points, any permutation of the cells and any public words, the polynomials the theorems assume exist, with exact vanishing polynomials. So `Params` and `Params.Exact` are not vacuous. The other hypotheses are satisfiable on paper and not exhibited in Lean: a pair of fields `K`, `E` of degree 3, a nonzero `van` vanishing on the rows, a satisfying witness, and the sizes of the `_bits` theorems.

**Tables as polynomials** (`Encoding.lean`, `Cantor.lean`). For a Cantor basis of `n` vectors (`beta 0 = 1` and `S(beta (k+1)) = beta k` below `n`, `S(x) = x^2 + x`; a finite field has one only up to the largest power of two dividing its degree, 64 for `GF(2^64)`; none is built in Lean for a concrete field, and the Rust test `transform_matches_the_basis` checks that the basis the prover uses is one) and tables of at most `n` variables, with a cell's point the sum of the basis vectors of its bits and digit `k` the `k`-fold `S`:

- `CantorBasis.digit_eq`: digit `k` of a cell is its bit `k` plus a function of its higher bits.
- `mle_eq_tablePoly_eval`: a multilinear claim on a table at the point `u_k = w_k(t) / (1 + w_k(t))` is the value at `t` of the table's polynomial (`tablePoly`, its entries on the products of digits) over the product of the `1 + w_k(t)`.
- `sum_tablePoly`: the claim at the point of all ones is the sum of the table's polynomial over the cells.
- `mle_snoc`: one more coordinate `e` of a table gives `(1 + e)` times its first half's claim plus `e` times its second's, which is the rule every claim's extra coordinates use.

**The four claims are the model's acceptance** (`Tables.lean`, `Assembly.lean`). `claims_iff_accepts`: let the cells be the points of a Cantor basis, the cells' vanishing polynomial its digit `w_m`, and the wiring polynomial the wiring table's. For a witness table of `m + 1` variables, a wiring table of `m` and a quotient table of `m + 6`, with entries in the large field (which covers tables over the small one), at a challenge where no `1 + w_k(t)` vanishes and the verifier's product is not zero, the four claims the verifier outputs (each table's multilinear extension at the verifier's point, equal to the verifier's value) hold exactly when `Params.Accepts` holds of the polynomials the tables hold (`witnessOfTable`, `quotientsOfTable`). Under it, one theorem per claim: `witness_claim`, `wiring_claim`, `quotient_mle`, `sum_mle`.

## The model, and what it leaves out

- **The protocol is stated on polynomials** (`Protocol.lean`). A table is read as the polynomials it holds, a claim on a table as an evaluation of them, and the claim at the fixed point as the sum of the low part of the sum's polynomial over the cells. `claims_iff_accepts` is the link from the claims on tables to this. Its points, scales and values are those of the Rust `reduce`, compared by review, and the tables' layout is that of `prove`.
- **`Params.Accepts` against the Rust verifier.** An independent review compared it with `reduce` formula by formula and found no mismatch. The model drops the scale factors of the claims, which are nonzero constants under the verifier's own check, and it does not reject a challenge with `1 + w_k(t) = 0`: it accepts more, which is safe for soundness, and differs from the real verdict on a public fraction of about `2^24 / q` of the challenges for completeness and zero knowledge. The Rust `verify` returns the four claims; "accepts" here means they are true of the committed polynomials.
- **Commitments are ideal.** For soundness the committed object is the polynomials themselves, of bounded degree, and they are what the hash absorbs: this is Fiat-Shamir soundness of the polynomial protocol, to which a real commitment adds its binding and extraction error and the hash chain its collisions. The extractor reads the cells off the proof, which in this model contains the polynomials; nothing is extracted from a commitment. For zero knowledge a commitment is a uniformly random handle and the verifier learns of the tables only whether its four claims are true, as one bit with its own check: the hiding of real commitments and openings is assumed. A real one needs the tables padded or masked, the quotient table included, which is not built.
- **The hash is a random oracle** on what the transcript has absorbed, one oracle with three kinds of input, so the chain cannot collide.
- **The adversary** of `knowledgeSoundness` is a computation over the oracle only, with no coins of its own; the bound extends to a randomized one by averaging, which is not stated.
- **Zero knowledge is for one proof**, from any oracle state. Several proofs follow by applying it in turn, each from the state the last one left, one more handle each; that composition is not stated. What a distinguisher asks after the proof is a function of the outcome, which carries the oracle's whole state; a distinguisher is not a formal object, and the bound is two inequalities for every event, which is a bound on the total variation distance, not stated as one. The witness is over `K`, with `E` any extension of degree 3. `Good`, the set of challenges at which the simulation is exact, depends on the witness. On a challenge the verifier rejects the Rust prover panics, and on one where a denominator vanishes on a cell it outputs a proof whose claims are false; the Lean experiment outputs a proof with a false verdict in both cases, all of them charged to the bound. The simulator is not shown to be efficient.
- **Soundness and zero knowledge are in two idealizations**: for soundness the hash absorbs the polynomials, for zero knowledge handles. No one game has both, so nothing is said of an adversary that sees simulated proofs and then forges. The oracle is this protocol's only: other uses of the same hash are outside the model.
- **The relation** is `Params.Sat`: the cells, elements of the large field, satisfy every gate, every copy constraint and the public words. Left to the circuit and not proved in Lean: that the rows, the wiring and the public weights of `quotient::circuit` satisfy `Params`' hypotheses; that the verifier fixes the last four public words to `(1, 0, 0, 0)`; that the Rust circuit is an `AdderCircuit` wired as `Circuit.lean` assumes, with its zero and one anchored in words 5 and 4: this is not proved in Lean, but the Rust test `membership_is_a_wired_loop_free_circuit` checks every hypothesis of `AdderCircuit` and `Wired` on the real circuit (input rows read their own output and two zeros; every other row reads constants or rows of lower rank, the public rows ranked last and read by none; every reading cell is on the cycle of the output cell it reads; the constants' cycles hold their anchors and no output; the statement's public cells read rows that drop the carry); that words 0 to 3 determine the statement's bits, their cells being wired to rows that drop the carry and their weights independent over GF(2); and that this circuit's computation is the BLAKE2s statement, which a Rust test checks on a random input. `cells_determined` puts `AdderCircuit.determined` on the protocol's cells: if every reading cell is taken by some power of the wiring to the output cell it reads (or to the cell holding the constant), cells satisfying the gates and the copies, with the zero and the one pinned, have outputs determined by the input rows. `Params` also has degenerate instances (no rows, no words), where the theorems hold and say little.
- **Soundness needs only polynomials that vanish** on the cells and the rows; completeness and zero knowledge need them exact (`Params.Exact`), and zero knowledge needs the mask's vanishing polynomial `van` nonzero and zero on the rows. `van` is in the row variable (in the protocol `w_20`, of degree `2^20`), not `Params.vRows`, which is in the cell variable.

## Files

`LogDerivative.lean`, `Counting.lean`: the copy argument and the counts of the first challenge. `Protocol.lean`: the protocol. `Cells.lean`: what a consistent second message forces on the cells. `RoundOne.lean`, `RoundTwo.lean`, `RoundThree.lean`: the three challenges. `Degrees.lean`: the degree of the identities. `Spec.lean`: the protocol, extractor and state function in ArkLib's types. `RoundByRound.lean`, `Bits.lean`: round-by-round knowledge soundness and 128 bits. `RandomOracle.lean`: knowledge soundness of the non-interactive proof. `Completeness.lean`: the honest prover. `ZeroKnowledge.lean`: the masks. `Simulation.lean`: the honest verifier's view. `NonInteractive.lean`, `Distance.lean`: zero knowledge of the non-interactive proof. `Circuit.lean`: what the relation means for a loop-free circuit. `Witness.lean`: every assignment is a first message's. `Instance.lean`: the parameters exist. `Encoding.lean`, `Cantor.lean`: tables as polynomials. `Tables.lean`, `Assembly.lean`: the four claims on tables are the model's acceptance.

Build with `lake exe cache get` once, then `lake build`.
