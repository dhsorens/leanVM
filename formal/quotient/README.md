# Security of `flock::quotient`

A Lean 4 development over [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib) and VCVio for the proof system of `crates/flock/src/quotient.rs`, described in `doc/quotient/main.tex`. No `sorry`; the theorems named below depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, which the root module pins.

## What is proved

**Knowledge soundness.**

- `knowledgeSoundness` (`RandomOracle.lean`): the non-interactive proof in the random-oracle model. An adversary makes at most `Q` adaptive queries and outputs a statement and a proof; the verifier asks its three challenges of the same oracle. The probability that the verifier accepts while the cells read off the proof's first message do not satisfy the relation is at most `(Q + 3)` times the largest round error. `knowledgeSoundness_bits`: at the protocol's sizes (`q = 2^192`, `N = 2^22` cells, eight words, identities of degree at most `2^27`) that is `(Q + 3) / 2^128`. It is proved from the three round bounds below and VCVio's bound on fresh bad queries (`prEvent_randomOracle_le_of_bad_queries`), not from the next theorem: ArkLib states the implication from round-by-round soundness to the Fiat-Shamir transform with `sorry`.
- `verifier_rbrKnowledgeSoundness` (`RoundByRound.lean`): the interactive protocol is round-by-round knowledge sound in ArkLib's definition (`Verifier.rbrKnowledgeSoundness`, through its worst-case form), with errors `(2 N^2 + 4 N + words) / q`, `d / q` and `3 / q` for its three challenges, each at most `2^-128` at those sizes (`rbrError_le`).

**Completeness, of the algebra.** `Params.honest_consistent`, `Params.accepts_of_consistent` (`Completeness.lean`): given a first message whose cells satisfy the relation, the honest second message (the interpolation of the sum's values and the two quotients) is consistent with it wherever no denominator vanishes on a cell, and the four claims are then true at every challenge the verifier does not reject. These are two lemmas, not ArkLib's `completeness`: there is no probability statement, the honest messages are not shown to lie within the degree bounds of the protocol whose soundness is proved, and that every satisfying assignment of the cells is the cells of some first message is not proved.

**Zero knowledge.**

- `zeroKnowledge` (`Distance.lean`): the non-interactive proof, in the random-oracle model with a simulator that programs the oracle. The oracle may already hold answers: `c₀` is any state, so whatever anyone asked before, and a statement and a witness chosen from it, are covered. The real experiment draws masks and two handles, runs the honest prover against the oracle in that state, and outputs the proof, its verdict, its challenges and the oracle's new state. The simulator `simNI` takes the statement and the oracle's state only: it draws handles, challenges and four values (uniform in the cubic extension `E`, or in `K = GF(2^64)` when the challenge's row is in `K`), takes the verifier's own nonzero check for the verdict, and writes its challenges in the oracle. For every event on the outcome, the two experiments' probabilities differ by at most `zkBound = (2 N + 4 deg) / q + |U| / |H|`: `N` cells, a vanishing polynomial of degree `deg`, `q` field elements, `U` the handles the oracle was already asked about with this statement, `H` the handles. `zkError_le`: the first term is at most `2^-128` at the protocol's sizes (it is about `2^-168`); the second is the chance a fresh commitment collides with an earlier query.
- Under it (`NonInteractive.lean`, `Simulation.lean`, `ZeroKnowledge.lean`): `realNI_eq_simNI`, the two experiments give exactly the same probability to every outcome whose first handle is fresh and whose challenges are good for the witness; `card_not_good_le`, few challenges are not good; `realView_eq_simView`, the honest verifier's whole view of the interactive proof is simulated; `realValues_eq_simValues`, three mask coefficients over `K` cover exactly one value in `E` (and one in `K`).

**The parameters exist.** `Params.ofData`, `Params.ofData_exact` (`Instance.lean`): from rows whose cells are distinct points, any permutation of the cells and any public words, the polynomials the theorems assume exist, with exact vanishing polynomials. So the hypotheses are not vacuous.

**Tables as polynomials, in part.** `Encoding.lean`: a multilinear claim on a coefficient table at `u_k = w_k / (1 + w_k)` is the polynomial's value over the product of the `1 + w_k` (`mle_eq_digitsEval_div`); the claim at the point of all ones is the last entry (`mle_ones`); and the last entry is the sum of the polynomial over the cells when digit `k` of a cell is its bit `k` plus a function of its higher bits (`sum_digitsEval`).

## The model, and what it leaves out

- **The protocol is stated on polynomials** (`Protocol.lean`). A table is read as the polynomials it holds, a claim on a table as an evaluation of them, and the claim at the fixed point as the sum of the low part of the sum's polynomial over the cells. `Encoding.lean` is not glued to this: not proved are that a Cantor basis gives triangular digits (`w_k(B(i)) = i_k + sum_{j > k} beta_{j-k} i_j`, which the Rust test `transform_matches_the_basis` checks), the rule for a table's free coordinates (the random `(rho, rho^2)`, the coordinate `w / (D D' + w)`, the two constant coordinates of `E` over `K`), and the layout of the quotient table in chunks.
- **`Params.Accepts` against the Rust verifier.** An independent review compared it with `reduce` formula by formula and found no mismatch. The model drops the scale factors of the claims, which are nonzero constants under the verifier's own check, and it does not reject a challenge with `1 + w_k(t) = 0`: it accepts more, which is safe for soundness, and differs from the real verdict on a public fraction of about `2^24 / q` of the challenges for completeness and zero knowledge. The Rust `verify` returns the four claims; "accepts" here means they are true of the committed polynomials.
- **Commitments are ideal.** For soundness the committed object is the polynomials themselves, of bounded degree, and they are what the hash absorbs: this is Fiat-Shamir soundness of the polynomial protocol, to which a real commitment adds its binding and extraction error and the hash chain its collisions. The extractor reads the cells off the proof, which in this model contains the polynomials; nothing is extracted from a commitment. For zero knowledge a commitment is a uniformly random handle and the verifier learns of the tables only whether its four claims are true, as one bit with its own check: the hiding of real commitments and openings is assumed. A real one needs the tables padded or masked, the quotient table included, which is not built.
- **The hash is a random oracle** on what the transcript has absorbed, one oracle with three kinds of input, so the chain cannot collide.
- **The adversary** of `knowledgeSoundness` is a computation over the oracle only, with no coins of its own; the bound extends to a randomized one by averaging, which is not stated.
- **Zero knowledge is for one proof**, from any oracle state. Several proofs follow by applying it in turn, each from the state the last one left; that composition is not stated. `Good`, the set of challenges at which the simulation is exact, depends on the witness. The Rust prover panics on a challenge the verifier rejects, where the Lean experiment outputs a rejected proof.
- **The relation** is `Params.Sat`: the cells, elements of the large field, satisfy every gate, every copy constraint and the public words. Left to the circuit and not proved in Lean: that the rows, the wiring and the public weights of `quotient::circuit` satisfy `Params`' hypotheses; that the verifier fixes the last four public words to `(1, 0, 0, 0)`; and that `Sat` for that circuit means the BLAKE2s statement (no loop in the wiring, the zero and the one, inputs made bits, the rows that drop a carry). `Params` also has degenerate instances (no rows, no words), where the theorems hold and say little.
- **Soundness needs only polynomials that vanish** on the cells and the rows; completeness and zero knowledge need them exact (`Params.Exact`), and zero knowledge needs the mask's vanishing polynomial `van` nonzero and zero on the rows. `van` is in the row variable (in the protocol `w_20`, of degree `2^20`), not `Params.vRows`, which is in the cell variable.

## Files

`LogDerivative.lean`, `Counting.lean`: the copy argument and the counts of the first challenge. `Protocol.lean`: the protocol. `Cells.lean`: what a consistent second message forces on the cells. `RoundOne.lean`, `RoundTwo.lean`, `RoundThree.lean`: the three challenges. `Degrees.lean`: the degree of the identities. `Spec.lean`: the protocol, extractor and state function in ArkLib's types. `RoundByRound.lean`, `Bits.lean`: round-by-round knowledge soundness and 128 bits. `RandomOracle.lean`: knowledge soundness of the non-interactive proof. `Completeness.lean`: the honest prover. `ZeroKnowledge.lean`: the masks. `Simulation.lean`: the honest verifier's view. `NonInteractive.lean`, `Distance.lean`: zero knowledge of the non-interactive proof. `Instance.lean`: the parameters exist. `Encoding.lean`: tables as polynomials.

Build with `lake exe cache get` once, then `lake build`.
