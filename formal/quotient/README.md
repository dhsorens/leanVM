# Security of `flock::quotient`

A Lean 4 development over [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib) for the proof system of `crates/flock/src/quotient.rs`, described in `doc/quotient/main.tex`.

**What is proved.**

- `QuotientSecurity.knowledgeSoundness` (`RandomOracle.lean`): knowledge soundness of the non-interactive proof in the random-oracle model. The transcript's hash is a random oracle on what it has absorbed, commitments are ideal, and the adversary makes at most `Q` adaptive queries before it outputs a statement and a proof. The probability that the verifier accepts while the cells read off the proof's first message do not satisfy the relation is at most `(Q + 3)` times the largest round error. The extractor is that reading: it is straight-line and needs neither the oracle nor a rewind.
- `QuotientSecurity.knowledgeSoundness_bits`: at the protocol's sizes (`q = 2^192`, `N = 2^22` cells, eight words, identities of degree at most `2^27`) that is `(Q + 3) / 2^128`.
- `QuotientSecurity.verifier_rbrKnowledgeSoundness` (`RoundByRound.lean`): the interactive protocol is round-by-round knowledge sound in ArkLib's definition (`Verifier.rbrKnowledgeSoundness`, through its worst-case form), with errors `(2 N^2 + 4 N + words) / q`, `d / q` and `3 / q` for its three challenges, each at most `2^-128` at those sizes (`rbrError_le`).

- Completeness (`Completeness.lean`): `Params.honest_consistent`, the honest second message (the interpolation of the sum's values and the two quotients) is consistent with a first message whose cells satisfy the relation, wherever no denominator vanishes on a cell; `Params.accepts_of_consistent`, the four claims are then true at every challenge the verifier does not reject.
- Zero knowledge of the non-interactive proof, in the random-oracle model with a programming simulator (`NonInteractive.lean`). Commitments are ideal and hiding: a commitment is a uniformly random handle, and the verifier learns of the tables only whether its claims are true. The real experiment runs the honest prover (row polynomials over `K = GF(2^64)`, each committed with the rows' vanishing polynomial times three uniform coefficients) against a lazily sampled oracle, and outputs the proof, the verdict, the challenges and the oracle's final table. The simulator `simNI` takes the statement only: it draws the handles, the challenges and four values (uniform in the cubic extension `E`, or in `K` when the challenge's row is in `K`), takes the verifier's own nonzero check for the verdict, and programs the oracle. `zeroKnowledge` (`Distance.lean`): for every event on that outcome, the two experiments' probabilities are within `zkError = (2 N + 4 deg) / q` of each other, `N` cells, a vanishing polynomial of degree `deg`, `q` field elements; `zkError_le`: at the protocol's sizes that is at most `2^-128` (it is below `2^-167`). The oracle's table is part of the outcome, which is what a later distinguisher shares with the experiment. Under it, `realNI_eq_simNI`: the two give every outcome whose challenges are good for the witness exactly the same probability, and `card_not_good_le`: few challenges are not good (a denominator vanishing on a cell, or `t` in a row that is a root of the vanishing polynomial).
- The steps under it: `realView_eq_simView` (`Simulation.lean`), the honest verifier's whole view of the interactive proof (five values and the verdict) is simulated; `realValues_uniform`, `baseValues_uniform`, `realValues_eq_simValues` (`ZeroKnowledge.lean`), three mask coefficients over `K` cover exactly one value in `E`.

The relation is `Params.Sat`: the cells satisfy every gate, every copy constraint and the public words. All of these depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, which the root module pins.

**The model.** The protocol is stated on polynomials (`Protocol.lean`): a table is read as the polynomials it holds, a claim on a table as an evaluation of them, and the claim at the fixed point as the sum of the low part of the sum's polynomial over the cells. A commitment is ideal: the prover's message is the polynomials, of bounded degree, and the verifier's four claims are checked against them (`Params.Accepts`). The fields are abstract: any finite field of characteristic 2 with an element `theta`, `theta^2 + theta + 1 = 0`, any set of rows whose cells are distinct points, any polynomials vanishing on the cells and on the rows, any permutation carried by a polynomial, any public weights that sum to the public words.

**What is not proved here.**

- Half of the encoding. `Encoding.lean` proves that a multilinear claim on a coefficient table at the point `u_k = w_k / (1 + w_k)` is the polynomial's value divided by the product of the `1 + w_k`, and that the claim at the point of all ones is the last entry. Not proved: that the digits `w_k` of the note's basis make these the polynomials of `Protocol.lean` (the novel basis of a Cantor basis), and that the last entry of a table is the sum of its polynomial over the cells.
- That the circuit of `quotient::circuit` satisfies `Params`' hypotheses, and that `Params.Sat` for it means the BLAKE2s statement.
- The hash as a random oracle is an assumption, and so is the ideal commitment: a real commitment adds its own binding error and its own extractor. ArkLib's general theorem from round-by-round soundness to the Fiat-Shamir transform is not proved there, so the random-oracle theorem here is proved directly, from the three round bounds and VCVio's bound on fresh bad queries (`prEvent_randomOracle_le_of_bad_queries`).
- For zero knowledge: the hiding commitment is ideal (a random handle, and claims answered by their truth), so the commitments' and openings' own hiding is assumed, not proved; a real one needs the tables padded or masked, the quotient table included, which is not built. The distinguisher is not a formal object: the theorem bounds every event on the joint outcome, the oracle's table included. A distinguisher that queried the oracle before the proof was made is not covered by the statement as it stands (the handles are fresh, so its earlier queries miss the proof's hash inputs except with the handles' collision probability, which is not formalized).

**Files.** `LogDerivative.lean`, `Counting.lean`: the copy argument and the counts of the first challenge. `Protocol.lean`: the protocol. `Cells.lean`: what a consistent second message forces on the cells. `RoundOne.lean`, `RoundTwo.lean`, `RoundThree.lean`: the three challenges. `Degrees.lean`: the degree of the identities. `Spec.lean`: the protocol, extractor and state function in ArkLib's types. `RoundByRound.lean`: round-by-round knowledge soundness. `Bits.lean`: 128 bits. `RandomOracle.lean`: the non-interactive proof in the random-oracle model. `Completeness.lean`: the honest prover. `ZeroKnowledge.lean`: the masks. `Simulation.lean`: the honest verifier's view. `NonInteractive.lean`, `Distance.lean`: zero knowledge of the non-interactive proof. `Encoding.lean`: a claim on a table as an evaluation.

Build with `lake exe cache get` once, then `lake build`.
